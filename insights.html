The empty row is the strongest number here. An instrument that rewards keeping things is one wrong word from reading as a coupon, and nobody made that mistake.

The one objection

  • “I don't like my spending being limited… the spend limits felt more like I'm being forced to look at specific items.”
1 of 7. At this sample that is one person, not a pattern. It is the only substantive criticism in the set and it points at the same gap as the weakest score.

What this supports

  • 7 of 7 described the rise correctly, unaided.
  • 0 of 7 read it as a discount.
  • The median answer on willingness to try a new category was strongly agree, the top of the five point scale, with 7 of 7 agreeing and 6 of them strongly.
  • 6 of 7 had previously skipped a purchase over return uncertainty, 5 of them more than once.
Two things make it worth more than its n: every tester got the identical task list, and C1 was scored against a rubric fixed before the responses were read.

What it does not support

  • That it increases exploration.
  • That it changes basket size.
  • That any of this holds at scale.
  • At n=7, self reported intent is directional. A signal to build the next test on, not a result.
